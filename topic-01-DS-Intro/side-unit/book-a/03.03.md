### Measures of Dispersion

**Dispersion refers to the degree of variation in the data, that is, the numerical spread of the data.**

For instance comparing the histograms of the facebook data clearly shows more variation in Friends than in the number of hours online/week.

![](./img/11.png)

##### Range
The range is the simplest measurement of dispersion. It is calculated as the difference between the maximum and the minimum value in the data set. Although Excel does not provide a range function you can use the combination of the MIN(data range) and MAX(data range) functions to get the answer.

##### Variance
A more commonly used measure of dispersion is the variance, whose calcualation depends on all the data. The larger the variance, the more the data are "spread out" from the mean, and the more variability one can expect in the observations.
The variance is essentially the average of the squared deviations from the mean.

The formula used to calculate the variance is different for populations and samples. All of the data we use in the labs and ca are samples.

The function VAR.S(data range) may be used to compute the sample variance.
The function VAR.P(data range) may be used to compute the population variance.

If we calculate the variance for the number of facebook friends:

~~~
=var.s(E4:E36)
~~~

The answer is 17996.8 !!

**A fundamental problem with variance is that it is in squared units so the answer is often very large compared to the mean.**

##### Standard Deviation
A more natural measure is the standard deviation, which is the square root of variance.

The Excel function STDEV.S(data range) calculates the standard deviation for a sample.
The Excel function STDEV.P(data range) calculates the standard deviation for a population.

~~~
=stdev.s(E4:E36)
~~~

The answer is 134.15 a much more understandable answer.

##### Chebyshev's Theorem
One of the most important results in statistics is Chebyshev's theorem.
For any set of data, a proportion of values lie within a certain number of standard deviations of the mean.
The empirical rules from this theorem may be applied as such:

- Approximately 68% of the observations will fall within one standard deviation of the mean.
- Approximately 95% of the observations will fall within two standard deviations of the mean.
- Approximately 99.7% of the observations will fall within three standard deviations of the mean.


![](./img/13.png)

Depending on the data the percentages may be slightly higher or lower.

For the facebook data, one standard deviation around the mean yields:

~~~
mean  number of friends  = 176.97
standard deviation number of friends = 134.15

One standard deviation below the mean: 176.97 - 134.15 = 42.82
One standard deviation above the mean: 176.97 + 134.15 = 311.12
~~~

If we count the number of observations that have number of friends in the range from 42.48 and 311.12 we find 25 out of the 33 observations lie in that area. Thus 75.8% of students fall within one standard deviation of the mean. This is different to Chebsyshev's rule, which demonstrates why it can be important to test the rule on your data.
